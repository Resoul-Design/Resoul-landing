Emotional support

Your grief is not only for a pet,
but for a family member.

Your grief reflects the love you shared.

The grief of losing a pet is real and deep. You may feel guilt, emptiness or sleeplessness, or feel that no one truly understands. Grief has no set timeline, and there's no need to rush to let go. One step at a time is enough — you don't have to figure everything out at once.

01

First, hold the loss

Sadness, panic, not wanting to let go — all because you love them so. These feelings don't need to be solved in a hurry; let yourself pause first.

02

Then, sort the next step

When your heart settles a little, slowly think about what comes next. One thing at a time — you don't have to make every decision at the hardest moment.

03

Finally, keep a memory

Give the longing a gentle place — at home and in your heart — so this love can keep you company.

About grief

Grief has no standard shape.

Some cry, some go numb, some blame themselves, some feel anger — all of it is normal. You don't have to be like anyone else, and you don't have to rush to feel better.

💧

Can't stop the tears

Tears are the language of longing; letting them out gives yourself a gentle release.

🌫️

Sudden numbness

"Feeling nothing" is also grief; you don't have to force yourself to be sad.

🤍

Constant self-blame

You did your best in a limited situation; this ache is proof of how much you love them.

🌊

Feeling angry

Anger — at fate, at yourself, at the world — is allowed. Emotions deserve to be seen.

Caring for yourself

Right now, you can be with yourself like this.

🫧

Breathe first

Give yourself a moment, sip some warm water, let your body slow down.

🗣️

Talk to someone

Is there someone you trust? Saying how you feel makes it a little lighter.

✍️

Write the longing

Write one thing you want to say to them, and give the longing a gentle outlet.

🌙

Rest when tired

Grief is exhausting; sleep when sleepy. You don't have to be strong all the time.

A gentle farewell guide

The Four Farewells

RESOUL has prepared a gentle farewell guide, so we can be beside you as you send your beloved on their final journey.

1

To thank

Thank them for every day together, and every bit of warmth they brought.

2

To love

Say the love in your heart to them, clearly and fully.

3

To apologise

Let go of regret, and forgive the self who already did their best.

4

To say goodbye

Gently say farewell, letting love continue in another form.

Take your time to gently sort through your emotions, and complete this final journey in your own way. You don't have to carry everything on your own.
Slowly, one sentence at a time, write it down or say it out loud. Let love find its outlet, and let your heart soften.
They were loved deeply by you; because of you, they lived a life full of happiness.
You're not alone. We're here with you.

If there are still words left unsaid, take your time to write them a letter — and print it out when it's ready.

Common feelings

How you feel right now,
perhaps it's here.

🌫️ Just faced loss, feeling lost

Pause first, let yourself breathe, write down the one thing you miss most, then slowly sort out farewell and memorial arrangements. You don't have to figure everything out at once.

🌧️ Self-blame, numbness & sorrow

Tears are a language of longing; they don't mean you're weak. Being able to cry gives you a release. Take your time — there's no rush to feel better.

Many owners feel this way. Perhaps remember first that you made decisions with limited information and did your best; this ache shows how much you cared.

Numbness is part of grief too; sometimes the heart presses pause for you. Give yourself more time and the feelings will slowly return. You did nothing wrong.

🕯️ Longing & remembrance

These small memories show just how close you were. Keep one or two of their belongings and gently take them out when needed, so the longing has a place to rest.

Those moments feel especially heavy, and that's normal. Prepare a gentle way to remember them — light a candle, write a few words — and slowly walk yourself through it.

They keep a gentle place in daily life for photos, jewellery and the memories that still matter, so the longing has somewhere to settle.

🌱 Moving forward slowly, finding support

This decision has no timetable and no right or wrong. Consider it slowly when you're ready; a new companion won't replace them — it's simply the start of another story.

Use simple, honest words, and allow them to be sad and to ask questions. Remembering the happy moments together helps everyone slowly come to terms with it.

Seeking professional help doesn't mean something is "wrong" with you; it means you're willing to care for yourself. If the sadness lasts a long time and affects daily life, contact the Mental Health Association of HK (2528 0196) or The Samaritans 24-hour hotline (2389 2222).

Professional referral

When you need more support,
we'll help you find the right person.

If grief keeps affecting your sleep, work, daily life or relationships, RESOUL can — with your consent — help refer you to a qualified and experienced counsellor, counselling psychologist, clinical psychologist or suitable organisation. Professionals assess, provide services and charge fees independently; we do not share your pet or health information without consent.

Crisis note

If you are having thoughts of harming yourself or others, cannot keep yourself safe, or are in immediate danger, please call 999, go to the nearest A&E, or contact a Hong Kong crisis service (The Samaritans 24h 2389 2222). This page and RESOUL's general care do not replace emergency mental-health or medical services.
